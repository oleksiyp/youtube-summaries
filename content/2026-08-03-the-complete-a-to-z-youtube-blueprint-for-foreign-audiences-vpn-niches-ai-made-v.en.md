# Complete A-to-Z Guide to Starting a YouTube Channel for International (Non-Russian) Audiences

This guide walks through the entire process of launching a new YouTube channel aimed at foreign audiences, from technical setup through niche selection and producing a first video using AI tools. It is designed for both complete beginners and experienced creators already monetizing channels abroad. The presenter shares personal experience, including a Shorts channel with over a billion views and a currently monetized American channel, as proof of the approach's effectiveness.

The process is broken into five major steps, plus bonus advice at the end: VPN and device setup, creating the channel, correctly configuring YouTube settings, choosing a niche, and producing the first video from scratch at minimal cost.

## Step 1: VPN and Devices

This step applies specifically to creators located in Russia who need a VPN to access YouTube; others can skip directly to channel creation.

### Why VPN Quality Matters

In the past, YouTube could be run for any target country without a VPN at all, because the platform automatically detects a video's language and distributes it to the appropriate country's recommendations — a Spanish-language video gets pushed to Spanish-speaking audiences, and this still works today. The real problem is **VPN quality**:

- **Cheap, shared VPNs** (e.g., around 150 rubles/month) often put 100+ users behind a single IP address. YouTube recognizes these spammed IPs and immediately flags the account as suspicious.
- **Dynamic IPs** that change frequently are also harmful — even if the IP itself is "clean," YouTube notices the constant switching and this damages account trust.

If you're confident your current VPN is clean and stable, you can keep using it; otherwise, follow the setup below.

### Setting Up a Reliable VPN

1. Register on a VPS/server provider site (the presenter uses Aeza, noting it's not a paid promotion).
2. Add a new service → choose a virtual server → select a server location based on lowest ping (e.g., Frankfurt).
3. Choose a payment plan — paying monthly is recommended, since **one IP address can safely host up to five YouTube accounts**, as long as they aren't all created at once. Stagger account creation (e.g., one account per week) so YouTube doesn't see ten new accounts suddenly appear on the same IP, which would lower trust.
4. After payment, wait about 15 minutes for the server to activate, then retrieve the IP address, login, and password.
5. Download **AmneziaVPN** (available on amnezia.org, with mobile versions on app stores).
6. In the app, add a new server, choose "self-hosted OpenVPN," and enter the IP, login, and password from your VPS provider.
7. Select automatic configuration and connect. This VPN can be run continuously (24/7).

### Verifying IP Quality

- Copy your VPN's IP address and check it on **Scam Analytics**.
- A **Fraud Score of around 3** indicates a clean IP that's safe to use.
- If the fraud indicator fills up to orange or red, contact your provider's support to request a replacement server.

### Device Considerations

YouTube can be managed from any device — Android, iPhone, iPad, Windows, or Mac. The only real risk is using a device already "spammed" with multiple accounts, especially if some of those accounts have been banned; in that case, switching to a clean device is safer. Otherwise, there's no major restriction — one example cited involves a network of 12 channels all run from a single computer on a single IP without issues.

## Step 2: Creating the YouTube Channel

1. Go to YouTube and click **Sign Up**, entering a Gmail address (you can skip adding a recovery address if prompted).
2. Once the channel is created, click your avatar → **Creator Studio** (YouTube Studio) to begin configuration.

## Step 3: Correctly Configuring YouTube Settings

Proper configuration is essential to help videos reach the right audience from the start, rather than sitting without traction for 2–3 months.

### Channel Settings

- Open **Settings → Channel** and set your **country of residence** to match your target audience — the United States for English-language content aimed at Americans, or Spain/Brazil for Spanish/Portuguese content.
- Add **keywords** associated with your channel's content (fill this in after selecting your niche).
- Under **Advanced Settings**, always mark the channel as **"not for kids,"** even if you plan to create children's content — this keeps RPM (revenue per thousand views) higher, since monetization rates are significantly lower on child-directed content.

### Feature Eligibility

- Navigate to the **Feature Eligibility** tab. The first feature is typically already enabled.
- To unlock the next two features, **verify your phone number** (choose your country, enter the number, confirm via SMS). If problems arise, virtual phone number services like OnlineSim can be used — you won't need the number again afterward, just enable two-factor authentication so the account retains verified status.
- After phone verification, video verification is often processed automatically, unlocking all three features.
- In the **upload defaults**, set the video language to match your target country (e.g., United States/English).

## Step 4: Choosing a Niche

Before diving into niche research, install the **vidIQ** browser extension from the Chrome Web Store.

### Understanding RPM and Niche Breadth

**RPM** (revenue per 1,000 views) is the amount YouTube pays advertisers' money out to creators, and it varies by audience: children's niches pay low RPM, while adult-oriented niches pay significantly more. RPM isn't fixed to a channel — it fluctuates depending on which audience segment your videos actually reach.

Niches also differ in breadth:

- **Narrow niches** (e.g., "making money online," with sub-niches like crypto income, YouTube income, or Telegram income) target a specific audience segment. These require significant experience and "watched-many-videos" intuition to succeed with, and it can take YouTube months to learn which specific audience your content belongs to — meaning a long wait (2–4 months) for a first breakout video.
- **Broad niches** are recommended for beginners because they're far simpler to break into. The goal is entertainment-style content understandable and appealing to the widest possible audience — both teenagers and adults — rather than content requiring a precisely targeted viewer segment.

### Finding Profitable Niches Using Data Tools

1. Go to **vidIQ.com** (or similar tool), log in, and skip any paid feature prompts.
2. Click **View Stats** → **Top Videos** → filter by **"Long"** format.

Long-form videos are recommended over Shorts because they generate significantly more revenue per view, and modern AI tools (including Claude) have advanced enough that nearly the entire production process can be automated, making long-form content not meaningfully harder to produce.

3. Apply filters for country and category (using YouTube's built-in content categories like films, animation, sports, etc.).
4. Browse trending videos within a category — for example, AI-generated animation content, such as a video with an AI-drawn girl thumbnail titled "I'll forget everything in 24 hours," created entirely using AI: AI voiceover, AI-generated images, AI animation, and simple editing in **CapCut**.
5. Check the channel's monetization and overall statistics — one example channel was shown earning roughly $230,000/month, though clearly operated by a full production team; this example is meant purely to illustrate what's achievable with AI-assisted content, not as a beginner-realistic target.

### Identifying "Indicator Channels" to Model

The key research method is scanning recommended/related content to find small, fast-growing channels worth modeling. Selection criteria for these **indicator channels**:

- Created recently — **no more than 2 months old**.
- Relatively few videos — anywhere from roughly 10 to 50 videos total.
- Most importantly, the channel must show **anomalous growth**: a brand-new channel whose view counts climb rapidly and erratically (e.g., starting at a few thousand views, then jumping to 120,000, then 500,000, then 250,000–320,000), rather than slow, steady growth.

Examples encountered during this research included channels with 193,000 and 316,000 subscribers, each with only around 30–35 videos, built on simple AI-generated illustrations (some styled like basic paint-program drawings) with AI voiceover, earning several thousand dollars per month despite relatively modest total view counts — illustrating that consistent niche performance, not just one viral hit, drives real income.

### Case Study: A Replicable Low-Effort Format

One channel earning **$5,800/month** was found to use an extremely minimal production process: AI-generated images are simply placed on a CapCut timeline in sequence with an AI voiceover track, with **no animation, no pause-trimming, and no background music** — about as low-effort as a video can get while still performing well.

By contrast, a similar video on a different, higher-performing channel (3 million views on the same topic, versus under 100,000 for the low-effort version) used noticeably better editing: sound effects, trimmed pauses, and constantly changing visuals every couple of seconds to maintain dynamic pacing. This comparison demonstrates that even modest editing improvements over a copied format can dramatically outperform the original — meaning beginners replicating a successful niche with slightly better execution have real upside.

## Step 5: Creating the First Video From Scratch

### Tools and Setup

- The recommended AI tool is **Claude** (the standard chat interface, not Claude Code, is sufficient for this workflow and is faster to use than ChatGPT, which requires much more back-and-forth explanation).
- A paid Claude subscription (around $20) is recommended over the free ChatGPT route to save time and effort.
- For those without a foreign-currency card, a virtual card service ("Pay The World" in the original context) can issue an inexpensive virtual card sufficient for paying for Claude, ChatGPT, and similar services, avoiding the steep markup (up to 50%) charged by typical third-party payment intermediaries.

### Production Workflow

1. **Transcribe the reference video**: Use a transcription tool (Turboscript) — paste the source video's link, select the audio language (English), and generate a transcript.
2. **Feed the transcript to Claude** using a prepared "master prompt" (shared by the presenter via their Telegram channel), selecting the **Opus** model for best results.
3. **Iterate on video ideas**: Claude will propose video concepts. If the initial ideas don't match the style of the channel being modeled, provide a screenshot of the reference channel's video titles and overall style, and ask Claude to regenerate ideas matching that specific theme and tone.
4. **Refine with screenshots**: Share screenshots of the reference channel's visuals so Claude can match the visual style, answering any clarifying questions it asks (e.g., about target video length — a short test length like one minute is reasonable for a first attempt — and script language).
5. **Generate the script**: Claude will produce a per-shot narration breakdown (useful for timing images to dialogue even without understanding English fluently) as well as a complete script for voiceover.

### Voiceover (ElevenLabs)

1. Log into **ElevenLabs**, which provides 10,000 free credits daily (additional free usage is possible by switching accounts if credits run out).
2. Go to **Voices**, filter by English, preview options, and select a preferred voice.
3. Set playback **speed to around 1.15x** to make the narrator sound faster-paced — this creates a more dynamic feel and **improves audience retention metrics**.
4. Paste in the script (noting a **5,000-character limit per generation** — longer scripts must be split into parts) and generate/download the audio.

### Image Generation

1. Return to Claude and ask it to generate image prompts matching the reference channel's visual style.
2. Use an AI image generation site requiring login; in its settings, select single-image output and the **"Nano Banana Pro"** model, which is sufficient for this purpose and faster than larger alternatives.
3. Paste each prompt from Claude to generate matching images, iterating with Claude (including sending screenshots of unsatisfactory results) until the visuals match the target style.
4. Download each finished image.

### Editing in CapCot

1. Import all generated images into **CapCut** in the correct sequence.
2. Add the ElevenLabs voiceover track, and expand the audio track's height (via the three-dot menu → track height) to make waveform editing easier.
3. **Trim out pauses** in the narration using the cut shortcut (Ctrl+B) at each pause point, then delete the silent gaps and join the remaining audio segments tightly together — this is essential for keeping the video dynamic and significantly boosts retention.
4. Trim each image's duration to align exactly with its corresponding spoken phrase, so visuals change in sync with the narration.
5. Add light motion — simple zooms and pans on each image — to add visual dynamism without much extra effort.
6. Optionally, add AI-generated animation effects (e.g., via a "Veo"-style animation tool on the same AI image platform) for additional polish.

### Results and Replication Strategy

Comparing the finished approach to the reference channel earning $5,800/month (with no music or animation at all) showed that even matching its basic structure — let alone improving on it — should be capable of achieving similar or better view counts. Thumbnails can also be generated via the same AI image tool by describing the desired style to Claude, which can likewise generate the video's title. In short, nearly the entire creative pipeline — ideas, script, visuals, voice, thumbnails, and titles — can be produced with AI assistance.

## Bonus Advice

- **Prefer Claude over ChatGPT for factual content.** If you run a broad, "educational"-style channel (such as the historical/science example used throughout this guide) and your AI-generated content includes fabricated or false information — a known risk with ChatGPT-style hallucinations — YouTube can revoke monetization entirely. Claude tends to ground its output in verifiable facts, and if monetization is ever mistakenly revoked, you can file an appeal using fact-based justification that Claude itself can help draft, since it retains the full conversation history and context.
- **Avoid long-term or complex niches as a beginner.** Start instead with a currently trending, short-lived niche (even one likely to fade within about two months) to build experience, skills, and some initial income before moving on to more ambitious projects.
- **Don't try to be too original without experience.** Beginners lacking a track record are unlikely to succeed by inventing something entirely new. Instead, find channels similar to the examples described in this guide and replicate their approach closely, adding only a modest degree of personal uniqueness. If you're not naturally creative, simply keep prompting Claude with feedback until it produces ideas you're genuinely excited about.
- **Example of a personalized niche twist**: rather than copying a historical-facts channel outright, one alternative idea discussed is building videos around plot summaries or standout moments from iconic TV series and films (e.g., *Breaking Bad*, *Dexter*). In this workflow, you don't need to write the script yourself — simply describe the concept to Claude and ask it to write a script based on that premise.
