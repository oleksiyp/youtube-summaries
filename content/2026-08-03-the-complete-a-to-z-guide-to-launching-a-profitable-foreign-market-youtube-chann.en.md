# Full A-to-Z Course: Starting a Foreign-Market YouTube Channel

This guide walks through building a new YouTube channel aimed at foreign (non-Russian-speaking) audiences from scratch — choosing a niche, setting up the channel correctly, and producing a first video with minimal cost using AI tools. It is designed to be useful both for complete beginners and for experienced creators who already monetize foreign YouTube channels. The creator sharing this guide has been working on YouTube since 2021, with notable results including a Shorts channel that passed 1 billion views and a monetized American channel.

The course is broken into five steps, plus a bonus section of practical tips.

## Step 1: VPN and Devices

This is a topic creators rarely discuss publicly, even though it has real pitfalls. People living outside Russia can skip this step entirely.

### Why VPN Quality Matters

Previously, YouTube could be used without any VPN at all — the platform automatically detects a video's language and pushes it to the matching regional audience (a Spanish-language video gets recommended to Spanish viewers, and so on). This still works today, but a major problem now is that many people use cheap VPNs (around 150 rubles/month) where over 100 users may share a single IP address. YouTube detects this IP spam and flags the account as suspicious.

Additionally, most budget VPNs use **dynamic IP addresses** that change frequently. Even if the IP itself is "clean," constant IP changes on an account damage its trust score with YouTube, since the platform notices the instability.

### Setting Up a Reliable VPN

For anyone unsure about their current VPN's quality, the recommended approach is:

1. Use a VPS provider (the example given is AEZA, not a paid promotion) to rent a **dedicated virtual server** with a static IP.
2. Register an account, choose a server location based on the lowest ping (e.g., Frankfurt).
3. Select a monthly billing plan rather than hourly — a single clean IP can safely support up to five YouTube accounts, but accounts should be created gradually (one now, another next week, etc.) rather than all at once, since creating many accounts simultaneously from one IP lowers trust.
4. After payment, wait about 15 minutes for the server to activate, then retrieve the IP address, login, and password.
5. Download **AmneziaVPN** (amnezia.org), install it, add a new server using the "self-host OpenVPN" option, and enter the server credentials to auto-configure the connection.

### Verifying IP Quality

To check whether an IP address is safe to use:

1. Copy the server's IP address.
2. Go to **Scam Analytics** and paste the IP in.
3. A **Fraud Score** of around 3 indicates a clean IP that's safe to use.
4. If the score bar fills into orange or red territory, contact the VPN provider's support to request a server replacement.

### Device Considerations

YouTube channels can be managed from any device — Android, iPhone, iPad, Windows, or Mac. The only real risk is using a device already loaded with multiple accounts, especially if some of those accounts have been banned; in that case, starting from a clean device is safer. Otherwise, there's little practical limitation — one acquaintance reportedly runs a network of 12 channels from a single computer and IP address without issues.

## Step 2: Creating the YouTube Channel

1. Open YouTube and click "Sign Up," entering a Gmail address.
2. Skip the optional address request.
3. Once the channel is created, click the avatar icon, then go to **Creator Studio** to begin configuration.

## Step 3: Configuring YouTube Settings Correctly

Proper configuration early on helps avoid 2–3 months of videos failing to find an audience.

1. In **Settings > Channel**, set the country of residence to match your target audience (e.g., United States for English content, or Spain/Brazil for those markets).
2. Add **keywords** associated with your channel's content (fill this in after choosing a niche).
3. Under **Advanced Settings**, mark the channel as **"not for kids."** This should be done even if the content will eventually target children, because it keeps the RPM (revenue per mille) higher.
4. In the **Feature Eligibility** tab, enable the two additional features beyond the default one. The second requires phone number verification — if SMS verification fails, a virtual number service like OnlineSim can be used. Enabling two-factor authentication removes the ongoing need for that phone number.
5. In the **Upload Defaults**, set the video language to match the target country (e.g., English for a US-facing channel).

## Step 4: Choosing a Niche

This is described as the most important step, and it starts with installing the **vidIQ** browser extension from the Chrome Web Store.

### Understanding RPM and Niche Types

**RPM** is the amount YouTube pays per 1,000 views, and it varies by audience: children's niches pay low RPM, while adult-oriented niches pay significantly higher. RPM isn't fixed to a channel — it shifts depending on which audience a given video actually reaches.

Niches come in two broad types:

- **Narrow/specific niches** (e.g., a channel about "making money online," with sub-niches like crypto income, YouTube income, or Telegram income) hit a well-defined audience segment precisely but are harder to break into without experience, since YouTube needs time to learn which specific audience your content suits. Breakout videos in this space can take 2–4 months to appear.
- **Broad/wide niches** are much easier for beginners, since the content isn't targeted at a narrow segment. The best starting niches are **entertainment-oriented content** understandable and appealing to the widest possible range of people — both teenagers and adults.

### Researching Niches with vidIQ

The process for finding a profitable niche:

1. Go to **vidiq.com**, log in, skip all paid upsells.
2. Click **"View Stats"** in the lower left, then select **"Top Videos"** and filter for **long-form** content (the guide focuses on long videos rather than Shorts, since long-form earns significantly more per view and, with AI tools, isn't much harder to produce).
3. Apply filters for country and category (using YouTube's own content categories — film, animation, sports, etc.).
4. Browse trending videos within a category to identify promising examples (e.g., an AI-generated animated video with an eye-catching thumbnail and title).
5. Open a channel's statistics directly from the results to see performance data.

### Identifying Profitable AI-Generated Channels

Several real examples were analyzed to illustrate the opportunity:

- One large channel earning roughly **$230,000/month** is run entirely with AI tools: AI-generated voiceover, AI-generated images, AI-animated visuals, and simple editing in CapCut. This is clearly a team-run operation, but illustrates the ceiling of what's possible.
- A smaller channel with 193,000 subscribers and only 33 videos (meaning it's at most ~2 months old) earns a modest amount so far because it only recently enabled monetization — despite already accumulating 15 million total views.
- A similar channel with 316,000 subscribers, 35 videos, and 27 million views uses simple AI-generated "paint-style" drawings with AI narration, earning around $2,700 — even though some individual videos only got 160,000 views, others hit 2, 5, or more million views.

### Criteria for Finding "Indicator" Channels

When building a list of reference/indicator channels to model, look for:

- Channels created recently — at most 2 months old.
- A small video count — roughly 10 to 50 videos total.
- **Anomalous growth**: a clear, sudden upward trend in view counts over a short time (e.g., 9,000 → 7,000 → 4,000 → 120,000 → 6,000 → 40,000 → 500,000 → 250,000 → 320,000), signaling that the channel has found a winning formula that's actively being rewarded by the algorithm.

### Case Study: Comparing Two Similar Videos

Sorting by popularity, a video titled "How Ancient People Actually Tamed Dogs" was used as an example. The creator simply generated AI images and dropped them directly into CapCut with a voiceover — no animation, no cutting of pauses in the narration, no background music. Despite its simplicity, this channel earns about **$5,800/month**.

By contrast, a similar (apparently copied) video "What Did Ancient People Do All Day?" earned 3 million views versus under 100,000 for the first version, despite being a near-copy. The difference came down to editing quality: sound effects, no dead air/pauses, and constant visual movement (something changing on screen every couple of seconds) made the second version far more dynamic and watchable. This demonstrates that **even copying an existing successful format but executing the edit better** can significantly outperform the original.

## Step 5: Creating the Video

### Tools and Setup

- The recommended AI tool for ideation and scriptwriting is **Claude** (the basic chat interface is sufficient — not necessarily the Claude Code coding tool, though that can be used later for automation). A $20/month subscription is enough, and Claude is considered more capable and reliable than ChatGPT for this purpose, especially because it sticks to factual accuracy better.
- For those in Russia without a foreign bank card, a virtual card service ("Pay The World") can issue an inexpensive virtual card suitable for paying for Claude, ChatGPT, and similar services, avoiding the high markups charged by other payment-forwarding services.

### Production Workflow

1. **Script ideation**: Use a prepared "master prompt" in Claude (model: Opus) to brainstorm video ideas.
2. **Transcription**: Use **Turboscript** to transcribe the audio/video of a reference channel's video (paste the video link, select the audio language, transcribe).
3. **Idea refinement**: Feed the transcript into Claude, review the generated ideas, and refine them by providing screenshots of the reference channel (titles, thumbnails, overall style) so Claude can match tone and topic more precisely.
4. **Scripting**: Once an idea is chosen, specify the desired video length (e.g., 1 minute for testing) and the voiceover language. Claude generates a per-shot narration script, which also makes it far easier to match each line of narration with a corresponding image during editing — useful even without strong English skills.
5. **Voiceover**: Use **ElevenLabs** (11 Labs), which provides 10,000 free credits daily (switching accounts allows unlimited free use). Choose a voice under the English language filter, set playback speed to around 1.15x for a punchier, more dynamic pace (this improves audience retention), paste in the script (max 5,000 characters per generation — split longer scripts into chunks), and generate/download the audio.
6. **Image generation**: Use an AI image generation site, logging into an account, copying the detailed image prompt that Claude provides (based on the reference channel's visual style), setting the output to single-image generation using the **Nano Banana Pro** model, and generating images one at a time. If a result isn't satisfactory, send Claude a screenshot describing exactly what's wrong until the desired look is achieved.
7. **Editing in CapCut**:
   - Import all generated images in the correct order onto the timeline.
   - Import the ElevenLabs voiceover track and stretch its track height for easier editing.
   - **Cut out pauses** in the narration using Ctrl+B to split and then delete silent gaps, pushing remaining clips together — this materially boosts retention by keeping the pacing tight.
   - Trim each image to match the exact duration of its corresponding line of narration, so visuals change in sync with what's being said.
   - Add light motion (zooms/pans) to each image to make the video feel more dynamic.
   - Optionally add animation via AI video tools (e.g., Veo) through the same image-generation site.
8. **Thumbnails and titles**: Thumbnails can also be generated using the Nano Banana model by sending Claude a reference screenshot and asking for a similar style; Claude can also generate the prompt and even write the video's title.

The guide stresses that a near-identical approach to the "ancient people" reference video — but with better editing (sound effects, tighter pacing, zero dead air) — could very plausibly match or exceed its ~565,000 monthly views and $5,800/month revenue, all using free or low-cost AI tools.

## Bonus Tips

- **Prefer Claude over ChatGPT** for ongoing content work: besides being more capable, Claude tends to ground factual/educational content in verifiable historical facts. This matters because YouTube can revoke monetization from channels producing "broad knowledge" content (e.g., science or history videos) that contain fabricated or false claims — something that happens more often with ChatGPT-generated scripts. If monetization is ever pulled, Claude's chat history can also help draft a well-supported appeal to YouTube support, since it remembers the full conversation and the facts it relied on.
- **Beginners should avoid long-term or highly complex niches** at first. It's better to build experience and a track record in a trending, even short-lived niche (one that might only stay hot for ~2 months) — this allows for some early income and valuable experience, even if the channel eventually fades.
- **Don't try to invent something entirely original without experience** — this rarely works for beginners. Instead, find channels similar to the examples shown, replicate their approach closely, and add only a small amount of personal uniqueness.
- For those lacking creative instincts, simply keep prompting Claude repeatedly until it produces ideas that feel genuinely compelling.
- As a concrete example of applying this method personally, the creator suggests a niche built around recreating plot summaries/storylines from iconic TV series and movies (e.g., *Breaking Bad*, *Dexter*) — taking a memorable plot and turning it into a script by simply describing the concept to Claude and asking it to write a script based on that premise.
