# Seven Financial Milestones You Should Reach Between 30 and 45

## Introduction

Financial expert Igor Shimko, with over twenty years of professional experience, argues that if you are between 30 and 45 years old and still lack a proper financial safety net, live paycheck to paycheck, and rely on consumer loans and credit cards, you have not yet learned how to manage money. This video outlines seven critical benchmarks that indicate whether you are truly ready to enter the next financial decade of your life.

## 1. No Consumer Debt — Only Cheap Mortgages

By age 45, your debt portfolio should contain no consumer loans at all. The only acceptable debt is a mortgage with an interest rate below 8%. The reasoning behind this threshold is rooted in how borrowing works:

- When you take out a loan, you are essentially pulling purchasing power from your future into the present.
- If you borrow at high interest rates, you are robbing your future self, because inflation (which in Russia has historically exceeded 8% for years) makes it nearly impossible to outpace expensive debt.
- Conversely, if you borrow below 8%, cheap money works in your favor: the asset you purchase (such as real estate) appreciates while your loan payment stays fixed and relatively cheap in real terms.
- Carrying consumer debt reflects a lack of financial discipline and self-control. The right response to wanting something you can't afford is to find additional income sources, not to borrow at high cost.

## 2. A Solid Emergency Fund

You need a safety cushion covering 6 to 12 months of expenses, depending on your employment situation and risk tolerance.

- **For employees:** Take your monthly essential spending (not income) and multiply it by 6–8, depending on how easily you could find a similar job and your personal anxiety level. For example, someone earning 100,000 rubles who saves 30% and spends 70,000 should multiply that 70,000 by 6–8 to determine their cushion size.
- **For entrepreneurs/business owners:** Six months is not enough, because business income is inherently unstable — banks themselves value entrepreneurial income less than salaried income because of this volatility. Instead of basing the cushion on total spending, isolate only the essential, non-negotiable expenses (excluding restaurants, discretionary purchases, etc.) and multiply that baseline by at least 12 months, since a full year may be needed to determine whether the business can recover or a new income source must be found.

## 3. The Ability to Save

By your mid-30s to 40s, you should have developed the habit of saving at least 30% of your income, more if possible. However, this should be pursued without extremism:

- Don't sacrifice your entire youth just to grow a brokerage account based on overly rigid saving philosophies.
- Equally foolish is the opposite extreme — buying a flagship luxury car on credit to impress others, then living on discount groceries and skimping on the car's own maintenance for years.
- Both extremes are forms of financial idiocy. The goal is balanced, sustainable saving — a fundamental skill everyone should master.

## 4. Building Real Capital

The natural next step after saving is accumulating actual capital — assets that generate economic benefit through dividends, coupons, or savings (such as avoiding rent by owning your home). To calculate your capital:

1. Add up all your bank account balances, checking accounts, and deposits across every institution.
2. Add the current market value of your securities portfolio.
3. Include harder-to-value assets: for a car, take the market price and discount it roughly 5% to avoid over-optimistic self-assessment; for real estate, discount overpriced local comparisons by 5–10%.
4. Include valuable personal items (luxury watches, jewelry, etc.) that could realistically be sold for a significant sum.
5. Subtract all debts — mortgage balances and any remaining consumer loans.

The resulting net figure is your true capital. It may be working efficiently or sitting idle — many people accumulate "sleeping" capital (unused assets, forgotten accounts) that functions like a hibernating bear, slowly losing value while still being usable if properly activated.

## 5. Knowing Where Your Money Is Invested

This is about conducting a full inventory of your assets — bank accounts, deposits, securities, real estate, vehicles, luxury goods, and more.

- If you cannot explain, in under a minute, to an average person or family member why you hold a particular asset and what purpose it serves, then you likely lack a coherent strategy for that asset — or for your finances overall.
- You need a system that performs reasonably well in growth periods, in stagnation, and in downturns/crises.
- Approaching your assets systematically and squeezing maximum efficiency from them is essential; many people own assets simply because they bought them, without any real purpose or strategy.

## 6. Making Your Capital Work for You

Your salary should not be your only source of financial well-being. Once you're financially stable, your capital should begin actively supporting you. The speaker shares his personal example:

- He holds dividend-paying stocks (including a favorite banking stock as his portfolio's flagship).
- He earns coupon income from government and corporate bonds.
- He maintains deposits as part of his safety cushion.
- He owns both residential and commercial real estate, generating rental income as part of his future retirement strategy.

Importantly, he emphasizes that he loves his work and has no intention of retiring early to relax on a beach — pointing out that people who dream only of quitting work rarely achieve much. Still, having capital that works independently of his labor provides peace of mind: if all other income streams disappeared tomorrow, his capital alone could sustain him. This security allows him to freely pursue what he's passionate about.

## 7. Having a Plan for Life After 50

You must already understand today how you will support yourself once you're over 50, because:

- Losing a good job with a good salary later in life makes it hard to adjust to lower income, and finding an equivalent position may be impossible.
- Beyond demographic shifts (shrinking population), there is global industrial stagnation and constant pressure toward cost optimization and automation.
- You may eventually become more expensive to employ than a 30-year-old equipped with AI tools that replicate — or exceed — your accumulated knowledge.

This is the harsh reality shaping the modern labor market, making it essential to plan now for how, where, and when you'll access money after 50.

## Conclusion: What Financial Maturity Really Means

Reaching age 45 doesn't require having tens of millions in net worth or an extravagant lifestyle portrayed in flashy social media content. What truly matters is:

- Having a functioning financial system and genuine financial literacy.
- Understanding that if you lack funds for something, you need to either find additional income or reallocate existing resources.
- Knowing exactly where you would turn for money if your regular income disappeared tomorrow.

This clarity and preparedness constitute true financial maturity. If you don't yet have these seven elements in place, the next 5–10 years should be dedicated to building your financial system, closing gaps, and strengthening weak areas.

Finally, the speaker urges viewers not to blame external circumstances or location for their financial situation — emphasizing that limitations exist primarily in one's mindset, not in one's environment. A financially mature adult takes action: seeking new knowledge, relocating if necessary, changing jobs, excelling at current work, adjusting business practices, and eliminating unproductive habits. Everything ultimately depends on personal initiative.
